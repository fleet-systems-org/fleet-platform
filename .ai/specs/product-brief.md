## Decision summary

The owner has decided what the first specification covers: the Release 1.0 table binds, and the first part to specify is pricing an offer together with the truck's next move; the next specification covers actual post-trip costs (E3/E4, D37); other Must items follow one by one in an order not yet set (Q18). This closes the question whether the 1.0 table binds (Q01).

The user is the granulate dispatcher. In his own account he answers an offer on the phone with a single rate per kilometre applied to loaded kilometres plus the unpaid approach, and plans each truck's next load around where it unloads (Problems, Key flows). The owner identifies the rate's source as his monthly summary, which shows revenue over all kilometres about equal to the division's cost per kilometre while revenue per loaded kilometre is clearly above it (Problems); the conversation and the summary both come through the owner, so they are not independent.

The first part is bounded by owner rules: a trip runs from the previous unloading to its own unloading and carries its whole approach, every kilometre belongs to one trip (R05), kilometres after the last unloading wait as an open approach until the next trip (R08), the wash question is mandatory and always a carrier cost (R09, superseding R06) and exact dates are required (R07). D32/R10, refined by D41/R16, additionally defines the future frozen month view; E01 stores timestamped segments but builds no month report. A quote shows the result of that one trip and where and when the truck becomes free, beside past similar trips; a verdict for a chain of trips is later (D22, D26). The dispatcher enters every truck's trips by hand, which is how the system knows each last unloading (D24). Per the owner the module has nothing in common with the company's office (document), messenger and warehouse systems, confirmed as no data exchange; read-only operational inputs and invoice history are read for E01 (D30, D28), while actual post-trip integration is later (E3/E4); all company modules are planned in this repository, added one by one (D20).

The belief most likely to change this: whether the dispatcher will use any tool at the moment of the offer. He has not seen a panel, the owner says he must be convinced, and his own remedy for idle trucks is more drivers (A01, A06). The agreed checks test exactly that: specify the whole E01 epic, check the specification with a synthetic-data mockup, then build in phases, starting with trip entry and the quote (D29). With that first running phase, he prices one real offer during the call, and we observe whether he completes it and what is missing (D21). One observation is a first check, not proof of regular use. Manual entry of every truck's trips adds to his effort (D24, A01).

The owner chose, against the agent's recommendation, to show past similar trips in the quote (D26). A data check found that a route can be read from nearly all invoice notes and many routes repeat, but the join of invoices with unloading, fuel and the approach was not measured (P11). So a similar trip is one with the same loading town → unloading town pair in the same direction, with person-confirmed spelling aliases (D35); the invoice history supplies its date and invoiced price, marked as an estimate, while results and empty kilometres come only from trips entered in the tool, which start empty (D28).

Two specifications ready for owner approval now record the owner’s E01 answers: [foundations](2026-10-26-e01-foundations.md) and [feature](2026-10-26-e01-trip-entry-and-quotation.md). Both are ready for owner approval, not approved or implemented. D38–D43 resolve financial input/weekend/month-split choices and the Europe/Warsaw weekend calendar. Only personal-email authentication method/provider (Q21) and late post-freeze evidence for E7 (Q24) remain deliberately open for these specifications. D42 resolves partial-day flat billing (Q25); D43 closes Q26.

Next: owner approval of both linked E01 specifications (D33, D29), linking the [older pricing review](../analysis/2026-10-26-older-pricing-review.md) (D10); produce its mockup in #21 to check the specification, then build in phases, trip entry and the quote first; the management interview (P8) stays open and matters for the monthly summary, which is not in the first part.

## Key flows

- Current state (dispatcher's account of one offer): a contact calls with a load → the dispatcher adds the empty approach to the loaded kilometres → quotes a rate per kilometre from the monthly summary → accepts; near the unloading region he looks for the next load among his own forwarders, avoiding regions that are hard to leave. `[INTERVIEW]` dispatcher conversation, 2026-10-23
- Current state (owner's account): the dispatcher says "let me check, I'll call back", checks his own way and accepts; the order then arrives by email, goes to the invoicing employee, who types it into the accounting program; accounting books it; the driver receives it by phone and a messenger location pin. `[INTERVIEW]` owner accounts, 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23
- Archived notes add: plan held in the dispatcher's head, profitability judged mentally, drivers send documents by messenger. `[DOCUMENT]` owner journey notes, 2026-10-18
- Future state: planning per D13; decided entry paths are E1 and E10a; every quote asks for the wash (R09) and the exact dates (R07); the owner's direction is a panel the dispatcher would want to use, designed from similar products, possibly with a map (Q11).
